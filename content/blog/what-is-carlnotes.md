Carl doesn't check the news. The news checks in with Carl.

CarlNotes is a topic-tracking app: you name the topics you care about, and Carl — the app's reader — checks their sources on a schedule, keeps what matters, and writes short ranked notes about each batch. It's free to start, with paid plans for more topics and more frequent reading.

Give Carl a topic — the framework you're betting on, the team you can't quit, the niche only you care about — and he reads its sources on a schedule: the open web, feeds, subreddits, channels. He keeps what matters, says why he kept each piece, and writes you a note about the batch. When you drop by, the pot is already on.

## What a topic looks like

A topic is a name, a prompt in your own words, and the sources Carl should read. He brews it daily or weekly, and every brew leaves a diary entry: what he read, what he kept, and what it cost. Findings arrive ranked, each with the reason it made the cut.

Share a topic publicly and anyone can follow it. Keep it private, or invite a few people by email and let them decide for themselves.

## Why notes instead of a feed

A feed hands you everything and makes the sorting your job. Carl does the sorting first and shows his work — every finding carries its why, and every brew carries a recap you can read in the time it takes to pour a cup.
