If you want to keep up with a topic, the usual tools are keyword alerts, a feed reader, or checking sites by hand. Here is where each one stops, and where CarlNotes picks up.

## Google Alerts: keyword matches, no judgment

Google Alerts emails you pages that match a keyword. It answers "did this string appear," not "is this worth your time." There is no ranking beyond recency, no reason attached to any result, and no way to say what you actually care about beyond the keywords themselves. Ambiguous terms flood you; narrow terms miss things said in other words.

## Feedly and RSS readers: everything, unsorted

A feed reader collects every item from the feeds you chose. The coverage is exact, but the sorting is your job: fifty items a day in, your attention out. Readers add folders and saved searches, but the unit of work is still you reading a list.

## Checking by hand: the default that doesn't scale

Most people track topics by revisiting the same sites and searches. It works for one topic and fails at three, and it silently narrows to the sources you remember to check.

## What CarlNotes does differently

A CarlNotes topic is a prompt in your own words plus sources — the open web, feeds, subreddits, channels. On a schedule, everything found is measured against the prompt: embeddings filter, models score, and only the best pieces are kept. Three differences matter in practice:

- **Judgment, not matching.** Results are kept for relevance to what you said you care about, not for containing a string.
- **Reasons attached.** Every kept finding carries a short explanation of why it made the cut, so you can disagree with the judgment instead of guessing at it.
- **Notes, not a pile.** Each run ends with a written recap of the batch. You read a paragraph first and drill into findings second.

## Where the others still win

Google Alerts is free and instant to set up. A feed reader is better when you genuinely want every item from a source, unfiltered. CarlNotes is for the middle case, which is most cases: more sources than you can read, and a clear idea of what matters to you.
