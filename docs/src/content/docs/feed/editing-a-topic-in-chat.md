Coffee Talk can change a topic, for a reader who may edit it: the topic's owner, and a team member
with edit rights on a team topic. Ask Carl to tighten the prompt, add a subreddit, or drop a source
that keeps bringing noise, and he proposes the exact change in words: the new prompt in full, or the
source he would add or remove, and why, naming the findings that make the case. Then he waits. Say
yes, and he saves it.

![The topic page with Carl's proposal in the panel, the reader's yes, Carl's confirmation, and the
toast that says the prompt was saved](../../../assets/screenshots/chat-carl-saved-toast.png)

## The toast is the confirmation

A change is saved when a toast says so: "Carl saved the new prompt", "Carl added reddit — r/austin",
"Carl removed rss — example.com". The topic page behind the panel reloads with the change. Without
the toast, nothing changed, whatever the conversation says. When a change can't be saved, a red toast says
what stopped it: the topic already reads that source, the topic holds all the sources it can, or the
plan says no.

## What Carl can change

Three things: the prompt, and the source list by adding or removing one source at a time. Everything
else about a topic, its title, schedule, visibility, and findings per brew, stays in the topic's
editor.

Adding a source comes with its projected cost per brew and per month at the topic's frequency, so
you know what one more place to read adds. A topic holds up to 10 sources.

## Nothing brews until you say so

Editing never starts a brew. The next scheduled brew reads the new prompt and sources, and your
existing findings stay put. To see a change at work sooner, press **Brew** on the topic page. Every
prompt save keeps a version.

## In a team's chat room

On a topic a team holds, a member who may edit it gets the same three changes in the team's chat
room. Everyone in the room reads the proposal, the yes, and Carl's confirmation, and the toast
reaches every member's panel. A member who may not edit keeps the read-only Carl they had. See
[Group chat with Carl](/docs/teams/group-chat/).

## Asking well

- **Ask for the exact wording.** "Propose a tighter prompt, two sentences, and show me the new
  text."
- **Ask him to cite.** "Which findings would that change have dropped?"
- **One change per yes.** Each proposal needs its own yes, and a bare "yes" is enough.
