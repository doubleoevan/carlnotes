Carl writes what he found. **Tasting Notes** are where you write what you think about it. Every
topic page has a Tasting Notes section, and every team page has the same section under the name
**Team notes**. A note is rich text with comments on it, and more than one person can be writing in
it at the same time.

The section lists what you can see as a table: the note's name, who may see it, and when it last
changed. Sort by any of the three. **Add note** opens a new one.

![The Tasting Notes table: each note's name, its visibility, and when it last
changed](../../../assets/screenshots/notes-section.png)

## Who may see it

Every note carries one visibility, set when you create it and changed later by whoever created it:

- **Private** — only you. Nobody else on the topic or the team ever sees it, and it is not visible
to your teammates whatever their role.
- **Team** — only the team holding the page.
- **Public** — everyone who can open the page.

Reading and writing are not the same rule. A private or team note is editable by anyone who can read
it, so a team note is genuinely shared work. A **public** note is readable by everyone but writable
only by the page's owner and the team holding it — the general public reads it and cannot change it.

Whoever created a note is the only one who can change its visibility or delete it, and deleting a
topic or a team takes its notes with it.

![The New note dialog: a name field and the visibility picker, which names who will be able to see
it](../../../assets/screenshots/notes-new-note.png)

## Writing together

Open a note and start typing. When a teammate has the same note open, their changes arrive as they
make them — there is nothing to save and no version to reconcile. The note keeps the formatting you
would expect of a document: headings, lists, quotes, code, and links.

![An open note: the formatting toolbar above the body, the visibility picker top-left, and the
comments pane beside it](../../../assets/screenshots/notes-note-open.png)

## Comments

Select any text in a note and comment on it. Comment threads hang off the passage they were written
about rather than piling up at the bottom, so a disagreement about one sentence stays attached to
that sentence. The speech-bubble button toggles between **Show comments** and **Hide comments** when
you want the prose on its own.

Type `@` inside a comment to mention someone. The list offers the active members of every team
holding the page. What tells them is the unread count below, which counts every comment on a note,
mentioned or not — there is no separate email for a mention.

## What changed since you last looked

A note you have not opened since somebody else touched it carries a count. The count is the edits
and comments waiting for you, and it appears in three places at once: on the note's own row, beside
the name of the topic or team holding it, and on your avatar in the header as the total across
everything.

Opening the note clears it. Your own edits and your own comments never count against you, and an
edited note counts once however many times it was edited.

## What notes are good for

The feed and [Coffee Talk](/docs/feed/chat/) are both about what Carl found. A note is the place for
what you decided:

- **A running verdict on a topic.** Which findings actually mattered, and why the rest did not.
- **The brief behind the prompt.** What this topic is meant to catch, so the next person to
[rewrite the prompt](/docs/topics/writing-a-prompt/) knows what it was aiming at.
- **A shared read of one week.** One team note per week, with everyone commenting in place, beats
the same conversation happening in five inboxes.

A note is yours to write. Carl does not read it, does not score it, and does not use it when he
brews. Context that should change how a topic is brewed belongs in
[Attachments](/docs/topics/attachments/) instead.
