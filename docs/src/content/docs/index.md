Carl reads the internet so you don't have to. You give him a topic you care about. He researches it, keeps what matters, 
and writes a note about each new finding that made the cut. You read the notes. You stay informed.

![The CarlNotes home page signed in: search, and your topics with their newest
findings](../../assets/screenshots/home-topic-feed.png)

Carl keeps only what's relevant to your prompt. He filters out everything else.

## Topic

A topic is a subject you give Carl to research. It has a title, a prompt in your own words, and an optional list
of sources to check. "Jobs I should apply to" is a topic. So is "the nocturnal habits of raccoons". 
Carl conducts research and builds everything from the topic.

## Source

A source is something a topic can read from. Carl's built-in web search is a source. So is an RSS feed, a
subreddit, a YouTube channel, a podcast, a Google News query, a Bluesky or X account, or a single
page you point him at with a URL. A topic holds up to 10 sources.

## Brew

A brew is one full research scan of a topic: Carl fetches every source, filters what he found against your
prompt, and only keeps the best findings. Brews run on the topic's schedule, or when you press the Brew
button.

## Finding

A finding is a resource that a brew discovered and kept: a link, plus a note from Carl on why it's relevant to your
topic. Findings are ranked and sorted by relevance, recency, or popularity. Each brew keeps the topic's top findings, from 5 to 20. 
This is your feed for a topic. 

## Attachment

An attachment is extra information you give Carl so he understands your prompt: a resume, a document, a list of
things you've already seen. Carl uploads topic attachments and reads them on every brew.
