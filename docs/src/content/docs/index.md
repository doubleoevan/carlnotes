Carl reads the internet so you don't have to. You give him a topic you care about. He researches it and keeps what
matches your prompt. He writes a note about each new finding he keeps. You read the notes and stay informed.

![The CarlNotes home page signed in: search, and your topics with their newest
findings](../../assets/screenshots/home-topic-feed.png)

Carl keeps only what's relevant to your prompt. He filters out everything else.

## Topic

A topic is a subject you give Carl to research. It has a title, a prompt in your own words, and an optional list of
sources to check. "Jobs I should apply to" is a topic. So is "the nocturnal habits of raccoons". Carl builds every
brew, finding, and note from the topic.

## Source

A source is a place a topic reads from. Carl's built-in web search is a source. So are an RSS feed (a page of updates
that a site publishes for apps to read), a subreddit, a YouTube channel or playlist, and a podcast. So are a news
publisher read through Google News, a Bluesky or X account, and a single page you give him by its URL. A topic holds up
to 10 sources.

## Brew

A brew is one full research scan of a topic. Carl fetches every source and filters what he found against your prompt.
Then he keeps the best findings. Brews run on the topic's schedule, or when you press the **Brew** button.

## Finding

A finding is a resource that a brew found and kept: a link, plus a note from Carl on why it's relevant to your topic.
Carl ranks findings by relevance. You can also sort them by recency or popularity. Each brew keeps the topic's top 5,
10, 15, or 20 findings. Together they make your feed for the topic.

## Attachment

An attachment is extra information you give Carl so he understands your prompt: a resume, a document, a list of things
you've already seen. Carl reads a topic's attachments on every brew.
