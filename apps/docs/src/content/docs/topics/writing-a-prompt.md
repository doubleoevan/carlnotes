The prompt decides everything Carl keeps or discards. Sources decide where he reads. The prompt decides what he looks
for and what he keeps. When findings look wrong, rewrite the prompt before you change anything else.

![The Edit your topic dialog with Carl's prompt front and
center](../../../assets/screenshots/prompt-editor.png)

## Say what you don't want

People skip this part, and it helps the most. Carl can only exclude what you tell him to exclude. Compare:

**Too broad:**

> Keep me up to date on CarlNotes.

**Better:**

> Keep me up to date on CarlNotes. I want releases, documentation changes, and anything the internet says about us.
> Exclude everything from the list of URLs that I've attached.

The first prompt puts everything about CarlNotes in front of Carl. The second gives Carl a way to filter out what you
don't need. To write your prompt with an expert, ask Carl. He read the documentation. All of it. See
[Making a topic with Coffee Talk](/docs/feed/making-a-topic-in-chat/).

![A chat conversation, where Carl suggests how to improve a topic
prompt](../../../assets/screenshots/prompt-chat-help.png)

## Name the content, not only the subject

Benchmarks, postmortems, teardowns, changelogs, job listings, and papers read differently from general commentary. Carl
can tell them apart. "Tech job market" gets you commentary. "New full-stack job listings, funding rounds, and published
salary bands" gets you three specific types of content.

## Say who you are

One line about who you are changes how Carl scores findings. "I'm a solo developer" makes enterprise purchasing news
irrelevant, with no exclusions needed. "I'm a beginner" moves introductions to the top. Carl scores against the prompt,
and who is asking is part of what makes a finding relevant.

## Longer context goes in an attachment

The prompt is the rule. An attachment is the background behind the rule. Attach a resume, a product spec, or a list of
things you've already seen instead of pasting it into the prompt. Carl reads attachments on every brew. A short prompt
keeps its rules clear. See [Attachments](/docs/topics/attachments/).

## Read Carl's notes as feedback

Every finding includes a note from Carl that explains why it matters. Click a finding to read Carl's summary of it and
why he picked it.

![A finding's popover, where Carl's Notes explains why the result made the
cut](../../../assets/screenshots/prompt-relevance-note.png)

## Edit it any time

You can change the prompt at any time. Open the topic's edit dialog with the pencil icon, rewrite **Carl's prompt**, and
save. The next brew uses the new version. Earlier findings stay in your **Brew diary**. You can also ask Carl in Coffee
Talk. He proposes the rewrite and saves it after your yes. See
[Editing a topic with Coffee Talk](/docs/feed/editing-a-topic-in-chat/).

To check a rewrite, run a brew with the **Brew** button. Compare it against the earlier entries in the **Brew diary** on
the topic page. The read counts, the kept counts, and the new findings show whether the rewrite helped.

## What rating means today

Each finding has a **Rate this finding** section with thumbs up and thumbs down buttons. Carl reads your thumbs-up
pages when he scores the next brew, and pages like them rank higher. A thumbs down hides the finding, and pages like it
rank lower. Ratings don't set the **Trending** order, which follows how many signed-in people opened each finding. The
prompt is still the main way you tell Carl what's relevant.
