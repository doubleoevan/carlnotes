If you can edit a topic, Coffee Talk can change it. The topic's owner can edit it, and so can a team member with edit
rights on a team topic. Ask Carl to tighten the prompt, add a subreddit, or drop a source that keeps bringing off-topic
pages. He proposes the exact change in words: the new prompt in full, or the source he would add or remove. He says why
and names the findings that make the case. Then he waits. Confirm, and he saves the change.

![The topic page with Carl's proposal in the panel, the reader's yes, Carl's confirmation, and the toast that says the
prompt was saved](../../../assets/screenshots/chat-carl-saved-toast.png)

## Where it lives

Pick **Edit topic** in the topic page's actions menu, and choose **Edit with Carl**. The panel opens on your private
chat about the topic, the same chat the panel's switcher offers. You can also open the panel and ask. A team topic's
chat room works the same way, for any member who can edit the topic.

The topic's card sits above the message box once an edit starts. That is the moment you pick **Edit with Carl**, or the
moment Carl answers a request with a proposal. The card updates with each save. It does not show while you only ask
about findings.

## On your phone

Tap **Coffee Talk** on the topic and ask Carl for a change in the private chat or Team room. His proposal opens the same
Topic draft card as the web, with the proposed values over the current settings. The card is a preview: answer Carl in
the conversation to confirm. A successful save shows a notice and reloads the Topic and card from the server. A rejected
save shows its reason and does not pretend the settings changed. You can dismiss the proposed preview without saving.

## The toast is the confirmation

A change is saved when a toast says so: "Carl saved the new prompt", "Carl added reddit — r/austin", "Carl removed rss —
example.com". The topic page behind the panel reloads with the change. Without the toast, nothing changed, whatever the
conversation says. If a change can't be saved, a red toast says why: the topic already reads that source, the topic
holds all the sources it can, or your plan does not allow it.

## What Carl can change

Three things:

- **The prompt.**
- **The source list**, one source added or removed at a time.
- **The settings**: the title, tags, visibility, how often it brews, the time and day it brews, how many findings a brew
  keeps, and whether its [Coffee Break podcast](/docs/feed/coffee-break/) is on.

When Carl proposes a new source, he shows its projected cost per brew and per month at the topic's frequency. You see
what one more source adds before you say yes. A topic holds up to 10 sources.

## Nothing brews until you say so

Editing never starts a brew. The next scheduled brew reads the new prompt and sources, and your existing findings stay.
To see a change at work sooner, press **Brew** on the topic page. Every prompt save keeps a version.

## In a team's chat room

On a topic a team holds, a member who can edit it gets the same three changes in the team's chat room. Everyone in the
room reads the proposal, the yes, and Carl's confirmation. The toast shows in every member's panel. Only the member Carl
proposed the change to can say yes, with their next message to him. Another member's yes saves nothing, so ask Carl
yourself if you want the change too. A member who can't edit keeps the read-only Carl they had. See [Group chat with
Carl](/docs/teams/group-chat/).

## Asking well

- **Ask for the exact wording.** "Propose a tighter prompt, two sentences, and show me the new text."
- **Ask him to cite.** "Which findings would that change have dropped?"
- **Answer in your next message.** Each proposal needs its own yes, in your own words: "yes", "sounds good", or "do it"
  all work. A question or a "maybe" saves nothing, and Carl asks again. In your private chat, only your own words count
  as a yes. Nothing in an attachment, an image, or a finding counts.
