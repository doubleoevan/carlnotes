## 1. Sign in

Go to [carlnotes.com](https://carlnotes.com) and pick a sign-in: **Continue with Google**, **Continue with GitHub**,
**Continue with Apple**, or **Continue with email**. Email sign-in uses a password. Carl sends your brew notes to your
account's email address. If you hide your email with Apple, your brew notes go to the private address Apple makes for
you, and Apple forwards them to your inbox.

In the CarlNotes app for iPhone and Android, the same account signs in: **Continue with Google**, **Continue with
Apple**, which opens Apple's own sheet on iPhone, or **Continue with email**. Creating an account by email in the app
asks for the same email and password, with no check to complete. The app's **Settings** screen signs you out and
closes your account, the same way the web's account page does.

Opened CarlNotes from a link inside another app, such as LinkedIn or Instagram? Then you're in that app's embedded
browser, and Google blocks sign-in there. CarlNotes detects this and shows the email form first, with a notice. On
Android, it adds a link that reopens the page in Chrome. On iPhone, use the in-app browser's own menu to open the page
in Safari, then sign in with Google there. Email, GitHub, and Apple sign-in work inside the embedded browser. Google
sign-in works only from a browser of your own.

## 2. Create a topic

Press **New Topic**, or pick **New topic** in the **⋮** actions menu on a page without that button. You can also ask
Carl. Open Coffee Talk, pick **Give Carl a topic. You know the one.**, and he builds the topic with you. See
[Making a topic with Coffee Talk](/docs/feed/making-a-topic-in-chat/).

The form is titled "Your new topic". Give the topic a **title** that describes it, not its category. Carl uses the title
as context, so "CarlNotes Documentation" beats "Documentation".

![The Your new topic form, filled with a title and a prompt above the default
schedule](../../assets/screenshots/quickstart-new-topic-form.png)

## 3. Write the prompt

The **Carl's prompt** field matters more than any other field. Every brew measures everything it finds against these
words. Here is a prompt to paste and adapt:

> Tell me about the latest and greatest features from carlnotes.com. What's just dropped. What's hot.
> Please exclude everything from the list of URLs that I've attached.

This prompt names the types of content it wants and says what to skip. A strong prompt also says who's asking.
[Writing a prompt](/docs/topics/writing-a-prompt/) explains how each of these changes what Carl keeps.

## 4. Add sources

Every new topic starts with one source: **web**, labeled "Let Carl crawl". It's Carl's own web search. That one source
is enough to start.

To give him more places to look, press **+ add a source** and pick a kind: **url**, **rss**, **Google News**,
**reddit**, **youtube**, **podcast**, **bluesky**, or **x**. Press **Recommend**, and Carl picks sources for you based
on your title and prompt. A topic can read from up to 10 sources.

Set the **Frequency** to how often the topic brews: **Daily**, **Weekdays**, or **Weekly**, at a time you pick. The
default is Weekly on Monday at 9:00 AM. Then press **Save**.

## 5. Read your first findings

Saving the topic starts its first brew. You land on your new topic page with **"Carl is Brewing…"** already running.

![The topic page right after saving: Carl is Brewing in the header, and the first findings already
arriving](../../assets/screenshots/quickstart-first-scan-running.png)

A brew takes a few minutes. When it finishes, the topic feed fills in. You get ranked findings, each with a note from
Carl on why it matters, and a recap of what he read and kept. A few minutes later, the brew's Coffee Break episode
appears above them. In it, Carl and Vienna talk the findings through.

![The finished first brew: its Coffee Break episode above the ranked findings, each finding tagged
with its chapter](../../assets/screenshots/quickstart-first-findings.png)

That's the loop. From here, Carl brews on the schedule. Each brew's findings arrive in your feed. If you leave emails
on, they also arrive in your inbox.
