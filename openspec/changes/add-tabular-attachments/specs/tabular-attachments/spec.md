## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: A tabular attachment is projected, never summarized

An attachment whose kind is tabular — `csv`, `tsv`, or `xlsx` — SHALL reach a Scan as its rows. Legacy `.xls` is out of scope: it is a binary BIFF workbook, not an OOXML zip, and the XLSX reader does not open one. Its context SHALL be computed by projection instead of generated by a model: a header line naming the file, the sheet, the row count, and the column names, then the header row and the data rows verbatim.

The header line SHALL be computed from the file, never generated. Every value in it is derivable from the file, so a model call would add cost, latency, and nondeterminism to produce a sentence already in hand, and the same file would stop producing the same context on every run.

The projection SHALL take no model call of any kind.

#### Scenario: A spreadsheet's rows reach the scan

- **WHEN** a CSV attachment is processed
- **THEN** its stored context includes the header row and its data rows verbatim, under a computed line naming the file, the row count, and the column names

#### Scenario: A projection costs no inference

- **WHEN** a tabular attachment is processed
- **THEN** no model is called for its context

### Requirement: A projection is limited by rows and names what it omitted

A projection SHALL be limited by a maximum row count, `MAX_TABLE_ROWS`, not by a character length. When rows are omitted, the projection SHALL end with a line naming how many.

`MAX_TABLE_ROWS` SHALL be a constant beside `MAX_PROCESS_CHARS`, not a plan attribute: the same file must produce the same context on every account, and plan differentiation lives in `monthlyBudgetCents` and scan frequency.

A projection SHALL also be bounded by a character budget, and SHALL stop before the row that would exceed it, so a wide sheet cannot spend an unbounded budget on long rows. Neither bound SHALL be the merged-context character slice in `finalizeAttachment`, which cuts mid-row.

#### Scenario: A long spreadsheet is limited by rows

- **WHEN** a spreadsheet with more rows than `MAX_TABLE_ROWS` is processed
- **THEN** its context includes `MAX_TABLE_ROWS` rows and a trailing line naming the number of rows omitted

#### Scenario: A projection is never cut mid-row

- **WHEN** a projection is stored
- **THEN** its final content line is a whole row or the omitted-row line, never a partial row

### Requirement: Every non-empty sheet is projected under one shared budget

A multi-sheet workbook SHALL project every non-empty sheet under its own heading, and the sheets SHALL share one row budget.

Projecting only the first sheet silently drops data when sheet one is a cover page or a pivot. A per-sheet limit multiplies the budget by the sheet count, so a workbook's cost would depend on how its author organized it.

#### Scenario: A two-sheet workbook projects both sheets

- **WHEN** a workbook with two non-empty sheets is processed
- **THEN** both sheets appear in the context, each under its own heading

#### Scenario: Sheets share one budget

- **WHEN** a workbook's sheets together exceed `MAX_TABLE_ROWS`
- **THEN** the total rows projected across all sheets does not exceed `MAX_TABLE_ROWS`

### Requirement: A tabular attachment is screened, and is failed when it cannot be

A tabular attachment SHALL be screened as untrusted text before its projection is stored, on the same path as any other document, so redaction runs over its cells.

The scanner fails open for every other input. A tabular attachment SHALL NOT be projected on an unscreened pass: when a scanner is configured and its screen does not complete, the attachment SHALL be failed with a reason naming that its contents could not be checked. A prose attachment survives an unscreened pass because the summarizer stands between it and the Scan prompt. A verbatim projection has no such step, and would put unchecked rows into the prompt on every Scan for as long as the file stays attached.

A deployment with no scanner url configured SHALL project normally. It has stated that it accepts unscreened text throughout, and failing every spreadsheet there would make the feature unavailable instead of defending anything.

The screen for a tabular attachment SHALL be given a longer timeout before that verdict, so a slow screen finishes instead of failing the file.

#### Scenario: A spreadsheet is screened before projection

- **WHEN** a tabular attachment is processed
- **THEN** its text is screened and the projection is built from the screened text, so redacted entities never reach a Scan

#### Scenario: An unscreenable spreadsheet is failed, not projected

- **WHEN** a scanner is configured and it is unreachable, errors, or times out while screening a tabular attachment
- **THEN** the attachment is failed with a reason naming that its contents could not be checked, and no context is stored

#### Scenario: A deployment without a scanner still projects

- **GIVEN** a deployment running with no scanner url configured
- **WHEN** a tabular attachment is processed
- **THEN** it is projected normally and is not failed

#### Scenario: Redaction preserves the table's shape

- **WHEN** the scanner redacts an entity inside a cell
- **THEN** the substitution is in place and the commas, newlines, and column alignment of the projection are unchanged
